# Skin barrier care

## Signs of a damaged barrier
A damaged skin barrier can cause stinging, tightness, flaking, redness, sudden sensitivity to products that were fine before, and more breakouts. It often follows over-exfoliation or using too many actives.

## Repairing the barrier
Simplify the routine: use a gentle cleanser, a ceramide or barrier-repair moisturizer and sunscreen. Pause exfoliating acids, retinoids and vitamin C until the skin feels normal again, which can take two to four weeks.

## Helpful barrier ingredients
Ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids, panthenol (vitamin B5), glycerin, hyaluronic acid, centella and niacinamide help support the barrier. Thick balms can be used at night.

## Dehydrated skin versus dry skin
Dry skin lacks oil and tends to be flaky and rough. Dehydrated skin lacks water and can be oily yet feel tight and dull. Humectants like hyaluronic acid and glycerin help dehydration, sealed in with a moisturizer.

## Patch testing
Before using a new product on the face, apply a small amount behind the ear or on the inner forearm once a day for three days. Stop if there is itching, swelling or redness.
